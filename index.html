AI Keyboard — Privacy Policy

Application ID: com.aikeyboard.app
Last updated: 4 September 2026

The short version. What you type is not collected, not stored and not sent anywhere. Four things do leave your phone, and only when you ask for them: the sentence you are working on when you tap an AI button, the message you are answering when you tap Reply (and, if you have switched Chat context on, the messages visible in that chat), a voice note when you tap Transcribe, and a promo code when you enter one. None of them is kept after it has been answered.

What the keyboard can see, and what it does with it

A keyboard has to receive what you type in order to type it. Android shows a warning about this when you enable any keyboard, and it is true of this one as much as any other.

There is no logging of what you type. There is no learning dictionary, no typing history and no cloud sync. Word suggestions, Hindi transliteration and the emoji suggestions are all worked out on the phone.

When text leaves your phone

Only when you tap one of the AI buttons — Fix, Rewrite, Friendly, Professional, Flirty, Hinglish, Translate, Reply, or one of your own tones. Nothing is sent while you are simply typing. No button, no transmission.

What is sent is the message you are working on, the button you pressed, and the random installation number described below. It is used to produce the answer, returned to you, and not kept.

Reply is slightly different, because a reply is about somebody else's message. It answers whatever you last copied — or, with Chat context switched on, the conversation on screen (see below). Before either is sent, the keyboard checks it on the phone for things that should never travel: anything that looks like a one-time code, a password or a PIN is refused outright, and card numbers and other long numbers are blanked out.

Where the AI buttons never work

The AI buttons are removed, not merely disabled, in:

  • password fields of every kind — text passwords, web passwords, the “show password” variety, and numeric PINs;
  • any field an app has marked as private, which is how a browser marks an incognito tab and how banking and health apps mark sensitive boxes.

In those boxes the toolbar says so and the buttons are gone. There is no setting to switch this off. Typing works normally; nothing is sent.

Voice typing

Tapping the microphone uses your phone's own speech recognition. On phones that support recognition on the device — most modern ones — the audio never leaves the phone and we never receive it. If your phone has no on-device recogniser and falls back to Google's, that audio is handled by Google under Google's policy, not ours. Either way the recording does not come to us and is not stored by this app.

The microphone is only ever live while the indicator says “Listening”, and it stops when you tap it again.

Transcribing a voice note

If you switch it on, the keyboard can turn a voice note you have been sent into text. This is the one feature where a recording leaves your phone, so it is worth being exact about it.

  • You choose the folder. Android asks you to pick the folder your chat app saves voice notes in, and nothing else on your phone becomes readable. You can take that permission back at any time, in the app's settings or Android's.
  • Nothing happens on its own. A note is only ever read after you tap Transcribe. The keyboard does not transcribe in the background.
  • The recording is not stored. It is sent to our server, turned into text, and deleted as soon as that is done — including when something goes wrong. It is never written to disk on our side, never backed up, and never sent to anyone else.
  • It is transcribed on our own server, by a speech model we run ourselves. The audio is not sent to Anthropic, Google, OpenAI or any other company.

Our server records that a transcription happened, how long the audio was and which language it turned out to be in. It does not record the audio or what was said.

Transcription currently works for English, Hindi, Spanish and Russian. If a note is in another language the keyboard says so and does not transcribe it.

Chat context (Chat Memory)

Chat context is off unless you switch it on yourself, in the app, and then grant it separately in Android's accessibility settings. It uses Android's accessibility service, which is a powerful permission, so here is exactly what it does, when, and where the text goes.

What it reads

  • The name of the chat you have open — the title at the top of the screen — in messaging apps only. That name is turned into an unreadable fingerprint on your phone and the name itself is thrown away. The fingerprint is used for one thing: remembering which writing tone you pinned to which conversation. It is never sent anywhere.
  • The messages visible on screen, only at the moment you tap Reply. The keyboard reads the chat once, right then, so the reply fits the conversation instead of answering one line out of context. It does not read while you type, while messages arrive, while you scroll, or in the background — there is no path in the app by which it can. It never sees your keystrokes.

Which apps

Only messaging apps, and the list is fixed inside the app: WhatsApp, WhatsApp Business, Instagram, Telegram, Signal, Messenger, Snapchat, Google Messages, Teams, Slack, Discord and LinkedIn. Android itself stops the service being told about any other app. Email apps are not on the list. Message reading currently works in WhatsApp, WhatsApp Business, Instagram and Telegram; in the others only the chat name is used.

Where the messages go

The messages read when you tap Reply are sent, over an encrypted connection, to our server, and our server passes them to Anthropic's Claude model, which writes the reply. That is the whole journey: your phone, our server, Anthropic, and back.

Before they leave the phone they are filtered: any message that looks like a one-time code, a password or a PIN is dropped entirely, and card numbers and other long numbers are blanked out. Only the most recent messages that fit are sent — at most fifteen, and never more than a few thousand characters.

What is stored

Nothing. The messages are not saved on your phone, not written to any log, and not stored by our server — they are used to write the reply and discarded with the request. Anthropic processes them to produce the answer under its own API terms and does not use them to train its models. The only thing the app keeps is the fingerprint-to-tone list described above, which contains no names and no messages.

Where it never works

Never in a password or PIN box, never in an app that is not on the list, and never unless you tapped Reply. If you would rather one particular reply used only what you copied, tap the chip that appears on the suggestion row and it does exactly that.

Turning it off

There are two switches, and they do different things.

Either switch stops it reading. Turn Chat context off in the app's settings, or turn the AI Keyboard service off in Android's accessibility settings: from that moment the app can no longer see which chat is open or read any message.

Only the in-app switch deletes what it remembered. Turning Chat context off in the app's settings also erases every chat fingerprint and pinned tone from your phone at once. Turning the service off in Android's accessibility settings does not: the fingerprints and pinned tones stay stored on your phone, unused, until you switch Chat context off in the app or uninstall it.

Clipboard history

Off by default. When you switch it on, the keyboard keeps a short list of things you have copied so you can paste them again. That list stays on your phone, is never sent anywhere, and is deliberately excluded from Android Backup so it does not end up in your Google Drive. Clearing it in the clipboard panel removes it.

The random installation number

The app generates a random number the first time it runs and sends it with each request. It exists for one reason: to count how many AI actions and transcriptions an install has used, so the free allowance can be applied.

It is not your advertising ID, your phone's serial number, your account, or anything else that identifies you or follows you between apps. Uninstalling the app discards it, and reinstalling produces a new one.

Your plan, and what the server keeps

Against that random number, our server stores only: which plan the install is on, how many AI actions and transcriptions it has used in the current period, and when that period resets. No messages, no audio, no names, no email address, no payment details.

If you enter a promo code, the code is sent to our server to be checked and the result recorded against that same random number. Nothing else about you is involved.

Who processes what

Rewrites, replies and translations are performed by Anthropic's Claude model. Our server passes the message — and, for a reply with Chat context on, the messages read from the chat — to Anthropic's API and returns the answer to you. Anthropic processes them to produce that answer and does not use them to train its models.

Voice notes are transcribed by a model running on our own server, and are not sent to Anthropic or to any other company.

Our server is hosted on Railway, which operates the infrastructure it runs on. Neither our server nor the app keeps your messages after answering.

Nothing is sold, rented, or shared with anyone else. Nothing is used for advertising, and nothing is used to build a profile of you.

What stays on your phone

Your settings never leave the device: the theme, the see-through level, the keyboard size and position, which languages are on, your own custom tones, which buttons are on the toolbar, the pinned tone, recent emoji, and the clipboard list if you switched it on.

If you have Android Backup switched on, Google may include your settings in your device backup, in the same way it does for other apps. The clipboard list is excluded from that backup on purpose.

Permissions

  • Internet — to reach our server when you tap an AI button or transcribe a voice note.
  • Microphone — only for voice typing, and only while you are holding the microphone on. The app cannot and does not record at any other time.
  • A folder you choose — only if you switch voice note transcription on, and only the folder you pick.
  • Accessibility — only if you switch Chat context on, and used only as described above: the chat name, and the messages on screen when you tap Reply.

The app asks for no other permissions. It cannot read your contacts, your location, your photos or your files.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

There are none. The app contains no analytics library, no advertising library, no crash reporter and no third-party trackers of any kind.

Children

AI Keyboard is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. Because the app has no accounts and collects no personal details, there is nothing to identify a child by.

Your choices and your rights

  • Stop text being sent at all — do not tap the AI buttons. The keyboard works fully without them.
  • Stop voice notes being read — turn Voice notes off in settings, which gives the folder permission back.
  • Stop Chat context reading — switch it off in the app, or turn the service off in Android's accessibility settings. Either one stops it.
  • Delete what Chat context remembered — switch it off in the app. Only that deletes the stored chat fingerprints and pinned tones; turning the service off in Android's accessibility settings leaves them on the phone.
  • Remove everything — uninstalling the app deletes every setting it stored and discards the random installation number.
  • Ask for deletion — write to heyshubigraphics@gmail.com. Since messages and recordings are never stored, the only thing that can exist is the usage count against your random number; tell us the number, or simply uninstall, and it is gone.
